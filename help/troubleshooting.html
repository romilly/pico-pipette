Troubleshooting

The connection checker tests each step and tells you what's wrong.

The list of ports is empty

  • Another browser tab or program (such as Thonny) may be using the Pico. Close it, then unplug the Pico and plug it back in.
  • The cable may only carry power. Try another one.
  • The Pico may not have MicroPython on it, or may be in BOOTSEL mode (showing up as a drive). See Putting MicroPython on your board.

The list doesn't include my board

Pipette lists only boards that identify themselves over USB as Raspberry Pi boards, which Picos running MicroPython do. A board built on the same chip may identify itself as its maker's instead, depending on its MicroPython, and then it won't appear. Pimoroni's boards, such as the Explorer and Presto, do appear. The connection checker uses the same list.

Pipette is for MicroPython, not CircuitPython, which is what most people use on Adafruit's boards.

The Pico is listed but won't connect

  • Close any other tab or program using it, then unplug it and plug it back in.
  • On Linux, your user may need permission to use serial ports. Run this, then log out and back in: 
    sudo usermod -aG dialout $USER

Connected, but programs don't run

The board may be running something other than MicroPython. The connection checker will tell you; if so, put MicroPython on it.

"Pico disconnected"

The Pico was unplugged or restarted. Plug it back in and click Connect again.
