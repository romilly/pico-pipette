Putting MicroPython on your board

Pipette needs MicroPython on your board. Boards built around the Pico's chip, such as Pimoroni's Explorer and Presto, usually come with it already. If yours does, you can skip this page.

Which MicroPython to use

  • A Raspberry Pi Pico or Pico 2: download the .uf2 file for your model from micropython.org. Each model (Pico, Pico W, Pico 2, Pico 2 W) has its own file.
  • Another board: use the MicroPython its maker publishes, which includes drivers for its display, buttons and so on. Pimoroni's are on the releases page of each board's GitHub repository.

Installing it

  1. Hold down the BOOTSEL button while you plug the board in. A drive called RPI-RP2 (or RP2350 for boards with the newer chip) appears on your computer.
  2. Copy the .uf2 file onto that drive. The drive disappears and the board restarts, running MicroPython.

This replaces the firmware the board was running before. Some makers publish two versions: one with their examples, which also replaces the files on the board, and one without.
